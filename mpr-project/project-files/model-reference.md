## Part 1 — Cross-family rules

These apply regardless of instrument and are the reason this file is consolidated rather than split by family.

### Reading a Yamaha model number

**Tier ladder (four-digit era):**

| Tier | Use case |
|---|---|
| 1000 | Budget student |
| 2000 | Standard student |
| 3000 | Upper student |
| 4000 | Intermediate / step-up |
| 8000–9000 | Professional |

**Three-digit models predate 1984** and fall outside Yamaha's modern parts kits. That's a serviceability problem, not a quality one — a good three-digit horn can be a fine buy for a player and a poor one for a fleet that needs parts in five years.

**Suffixes:** `S` = silver-plate · `M` = matte/other finish · `N` = nickel · `H` = B-foot (flute) · `D` = detachable bell (horn) · `II` = second-generation revision of the same model.

### The finish trap

**Silver-plate is a hard program requirement. Lacquer is rejected.** The finish claim in a listing title is therefore a filter — and it is the claim most often wrong.

**"Silver" in a listing frequently means nickel-plated**, especially from Japanese sellers translating loosely. Confirmed nickel despite the label:

- **YTR-135**
- **YTR-136**
- **YTR-1310**

Nickel is harder, colder-looking, and doesn't tarnish the way silver does. Where plating has worn through at contact points, the base metal colour tells the truth. Check the model number against the `S` suffix and check the wear pattern in the photographs — never take the word.

### Japan-stamped provenance

Yamaha moved student brass production to Chinese factories around **March 2012**. A pre-2012 Japan stamp carries a genuine premium and is worth paying for — **but only if a photograph shows the stamp.** Seller location is not provenance.

### Sticky valves are almost never compression

A horn that sits unused gums up: oil evaporates, residue dries. **Heavier-viscosity valve oil solves it**, and has solved it in this program before — Janice's YTR-2330S. Try the oil before booking service, and certainly before treating a sticky valve as a reason to buy a replacement. Genuine compression loss is diagnosed on a bench, not from a listing.

Currently open: **MPR-007** (Kevin H., YTR-1310) flagged sticky, heavier oil not yet tried.

### Grey market

Grey-market imports carry no US warranty. Acceptable at student-tier prices, and worth knowing before it's discovered at a repair shop rather than after. Janice's YTR-2330S is a Korean-market import.

**Configuration trade-offs:**

| Attribute | Options | Practical impact |
|---|---|---|
| Footjoint | C-foot vs **B-foot** | B-foot extends the low range and gives more long-term flexibility |
| G key | **Offset G** vs inline | Offset preferred for ergonomics; teacher confirmed not strictly required |
| Toneholes | Closed vs **open hole** | Open hole supports technique for a student progressing into high school |

**Case replacement** (post-purchase accessory, prices Apr 2026): Gator GC-FLUTE ABS ~$60–80, best overall protection · MTS 810E ~$40–50, budget · Kaces lightweight hardshell ~$40–70 · used OEM Yamaha ~$30–50, cheapest with condition risk. All confirmed to fit B-foot.
