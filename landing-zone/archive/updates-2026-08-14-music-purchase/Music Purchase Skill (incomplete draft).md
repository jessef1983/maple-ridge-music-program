# Music Purchase

Find music **for this band**, not merely music that looks appropriate in a catalog.

The goal is to build a useful repertoire library while avoiding the most common purchasing failure: buying a piece because it is labeled Grade 2, Grade 3, or “easy,” only to discover that the actual ensemble cannot cover the instrumentation, ranges, parts, or musical demands.

The current students and their actual assignments are the starting point. Catalog grade is evidence, not the final decision.

## Core principle

> **The ensemble determines the repertoire. The catalog determines the candidates.**

A Grade 2 piece with an exposed horn part may be a poor choice for this year's band. A Grade 2.5 piece with strong doubling, sensible ranges, and a good part for the actual students may be an excellent choice.

Every recommendation should answer:

1. Who will play it?
2. What instruments does the score actually require?
3. What parts are exposed or difficult?
4. What is the true playing level of this ensemble?
5. What musical or technical skill does the piece develop?
6. Does the piece fill a repertoire need?
7. Is it worth spending the program's money on?

### Historical student/repertoire data

Older roster and NYSSMA data may be useful for understanding:

- approximate student ability
- previous repertoire
- previous solo levels
- instruments that have historically been strong or weak
- repertoire students have already encountered

But historical data is **not evidence of the current ensemble**.

If an old student appears in historical data but not in the current roster, do not assume they are still present.

The handoff is the **purchase decision**.

This skill should produce a confident shortlist such as:

> **BUY NOW:** excellent fit, fills a repertoire gap, appropriate for current students  
> **BUY / IF BUDGET:** strong fit but less urgent  
> **WATCH:** promising but one part or student is currently questionable  
> **PASS:** wrong instrumentation, level, or musical value
